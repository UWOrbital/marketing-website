## Design Specificity Verdict

**The visual world is authored. The evidence inside it is interchangeable.**

The typographic system is real design work. Six type steps, one 2fr/6fr/4fr grid repeated from the page head to the footer, the hairline as the only divider, a drawn arrow instead of a Unicode glyph, and a scroll-timeline field whose clear points were measured in pixels and written down. A person made those decisions and defended them.

The content layer is not authored. All six subteam pages, the three landing tiles, and the five tiles at the foot of every subteam page carry NASA and ESA press images. A student who clicks Mechanical to learn what Mechanical does receives the Pillars of Creation. Meanwhile 13 photographs of students soldering, of the chassis on a shaker table, and of the flight unit in its case sit on the third nav item, with empty alt text and no captions.

Swap the wordmark and the six subteam names and this site fits any CubeSat team in North America. That single fact causes the "empty" and "repetitive" complaints.

**Deterministic scan:** detect.mjs returned exit 0 and an empty findings array on src/pages.tsx, src/ui.tsx and src/App.tsx. Zero rules triggered. The mechanical floor is clean, which is why the remaining problems are all judgment and evidence problems.

**Visual overlays:** not attempted. No user-visible overlay exists for this run.

## Overall Impression

The craft is real and the emptiness is structural, not atmospheric. Three specific decisions cause almost everything the complaint list names, and none of them is the starfield.

## What's Working

1. **The grid is visible.** Because .page excludes the margin, every hairline on the site lands on the same two vertical lines across nine routes. That is why the site reads as one object.
2. **The field is engineered.** It animates opacity and transform only, on a scroll timeline, so it runs off the main thread. Someone measured secondary-text contrast against the sky and wrote the number down.
3. **The lightbox uses the platform.** A native dialog with showModal gives Esc, backdrop dismiss, focus trap and focus return in 14 lines.
4. **Accessibility mechanics are better than they look.** Exactly one h1 per route, no skipped heading levels, no img missing its alt attribute, a working skip link, and a visible focus ring on all 14 tab stops of all 7 routes. The mobile menu toggles aria-expanded correctly.

## Priority Issues

### P0 The header is invisible over content, on every route

Measured with the header hidden and the real pixels behind the nav sampled. On /team at scrollY 1314 the mean contrast is 1.12:1 and 100 percent of the nav area falls below 3:1. On /sponsors the white logo sits on a white sponsor sheet at 1.20:1 across 88.9 percent of its box. All 7 routes have at least one reachable scroll position below 4.5:1.

It is worse than reported. The bar also overprints the site's own display type. At Mission scroll 2700 the logo sits on top of the word "Competition".

Fix inside the world: no panel. Give the bar a state that appears only when it must. Paint a near-ground wash with a hairline bottom edge once the head has scrolled past, and lift nav links from --text-2 to --text in that state. Nothing appears while the reader is on the deep field, which is where a transparent bar earns its keep.

Command: /impeccable adapt

### P0 Every page holds a full viewport for one word

All eight pages pass `stage` to PageHead. The component's own doc comment at src/ui.tsx:45 says "The landing page only." Measured ink coverage of the first screen: 4 percent on Team, 6 percent on Mission, against 26 percent on Home.

This is the root cause of both "empty" and "repetitive". Every page opens identically, so the background is the only thing in view, so the background is what gets blamed. Different starfield crops do not fix it. I cut and compared six; they all read as black with stars.

Fix: keep `stage` on Home, where arrival earns it. Drop it on the other seven and let each page's own material lead. Gallery opens on photographs. Team opens on the team photograph. Sponsors opens on the ask.

Command: /impeccable layout

### P0 Stock astronomy stands in for the team's own hardware

Six Webb files carry the whole photographic burden of /team, all six subteam pages, and the landing Subteams section. The team's 13 real photographs and 3 CAD renders appear on /gallery and / only.

For a student, a nebula does not show what Mechanical does on a Tuesday. For a sponsor, a nebula is not proof; a chassis bolted to a vibration fixture is, and PRODUCT.md names that as the team's strongest claim.

Fix: change src values only. No visual grammar changes. Point teams[].image at the closest real photograph, caption the gallery, and keep the Webb images for what they are good at, which is the field behind type.

Command: /impeccable layout

### P1 Two signal-red fills sit on every page

src/ui.tsx states the rule: one red button per page. src/index.css repeats it. The header renders a red "Join us" on every route, and every page then adds its own red button. On /team a hovered row adds a third red disc. On /join the header's red button links to the page the reader is already on.

Red is the site's only fill and its entire persuasion budget. Spending it twice per screen empties it.

Fix: make the header CTA a bordered outline button. Keep red for the one in-page action.

Command: /impeccable colorize

### P1 The arrow both overflows and sits far from its link

Two separate defects behind one complaint.

The overflow is real and measured. At rest the arrow's right edge is flush with the grid line. On hover the 4px travel is unabsorbed, so it lands 3.96 to 4.00 px outside the grid on rows, tiles and section links. It never reaches the window edge, so there is no sideways scroll.

The distance is worse than the overflow. The row grid gives 4fr to a trailing column that holds only an 18px arrow. On /team at 1440 the title ends near x=850 and the arrow sits at x=1361. On a phone the arrow drops onto its own line below the summary.

Fix: absorb the travel by resting the arrow 4px inside and landing it on the grid line on hover. Separately, decide whether the trailing column earns its width or should carry a fact.

Command: /impeccable polish

## Persona Red Flags

**A first-year at a club fair, on a phone, 20 seconds.** The first screen holds stars, a Menu box, and a wordmark not legible at 22px. No red button, no team name in words. The headline sits 950px down a 1780px stage. To act she must open the menu, because the header CTA is hidden below 900px. ## Copy That Reads As Written By A Model

Checked mechanically, not by feel. Every string was compared against the captured Wix archive; anything absent from it was written during the rebuild.

1. **mission.statement asserts a fact that is not one.** "CUBICS pays for the launch." The proposal is not submitted, the deadline is 19 November 2026, and no outcome exists. PRODUCT.md principle 2 forbids implying a launch the team has not received. This is a correctness problem, not a style problem, and it is the one I would fix regardless of taste.
2. **The landing lede says launch twice in 20 words** and stitches two claims with the "X, to make it the Y" appositive.
3. **All six subteam summaries share one mould.** Present-tense verb, object, one relative clause, 8 to 13 words, no proper noun, no number. Individually two are good. Together the uniformity is the tell. Business, "Funds the project and tells people it exists," is the clearest.
4. **The Software body and stack are already marked PLACEHOLDER** in the file and await lead confirmation.
5. **"Now we fly it." contradicts "The launch date is TBD."** A CUBICS reviewer will read both pages.

Weakest copy on the site is not machine-written. sponsorIntro and join.why are legacy Wix marketing filler, and they are the first paragraph each audience reads.
